Roof Leak Water Damage in Athens: What to Know

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. A loose shingle, a failed flashing joint or an aging seal around a skylight lets rain in, and it travels across the roof deck and framing until it falls into the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. Inside the home, our team takes out saturated insulation, dries the wood and drywall, treats any mold and repairs the ceiling. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Catch the DripsFurniture, electronics and rugs should be moved aside, with buckets placed wherever water is falling. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Document What You SeeBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Aging, cracked seals around a skylight frame that no longer keep water out.
  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • Wind-torn, curling or split shingles that leave the underlayment open to rain.
  • Water backing up at the eave and under the shingles because the gutters are blocked.
  • Sun-cracked rubber collars where plumbing vent pipes come through the roof.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Drywall Ceilings

Staining, sagging and loss of strength follow when drywall is not dried. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Hidden Attic Mold

Damp framing, paper-faced drywall, warmth and darkness give mold what it needs. Within 24 to 48 hours it can start growing, then move across the bottom of the roof deck.

Attic Insulation

For days after a leak, matted insulation sits wet on top of the ceiling and no longer insulates. Drying is sometimes possible with fiberglass. Soaked cellulose usually has to be removed.

Ceiling Fixtures and Wiring

Recessed cans, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are low points where roof water collects and drips through. Keep that circuit off until someone has inspected it, since damp wiring may corrode or short out.
