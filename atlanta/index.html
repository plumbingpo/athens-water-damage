Simple Ways to Keep an Atlanta Home Dry

Water damage looks sudden, but the causes are usually slow: a hose that aged, a seal that dried out, a drain that clogged a little more each month. For homeowners in Atlanta, prevention is mainly a matter of knowing where to look.

Plumbing comes first. Watch your water bill and your water pressure, because an unexplained rise in either can point to a hidden leak or stress on the pipes. Appliances are second. What Usually Goes Wrong in Atlanta Homes

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for Atlanta households. They are all common, and with every one of them waiting adds to the repair.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Fans and towels dry what you can see, while the wall cavity, subfloor and carpet pad stay damp underneath. Since colonies can form in 24 to 48 hours, the first hint is often an earthy smell or speckled drywall weeks later.

Hoses: Every sink, toilet and washer is fed by a flexible connector that holds full house pressure around the clock. A burst connector keeps spraying until somebody closes a valve, and that may take a long while in an empty house. The low-cost defense is to swap out aging hoses and learn where the main shutoff is.

Pumps: In homes that have one, a sump pump works unnoticed for years and then quits on the one night it is needed. Stuck float switches, tripped breakers and burned-out motors are what we find most. That leaves the lowest level under a few inches of water, with flooring, wall bases and stored belongings soaked. A battery backup and a quick test before the rainy part of the year go a long way.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in Atlanta, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. Clean water from the tank or supply is one thing, but water from the bowl after use is contaminated. In both cases it seeps beneath cabinets and flooring edges, and a moisture meter shows how far it went.

Cooling: Few people think about the small pipe that carries condensation away from an AC system. A rusted pan or a plugged line lets that water run into nearby flooring or the ceiling below. The leak is gradual, so drywall and insulation may be soaked long before you spot a mark. Most of these are avoided by getting the drain cleaned annually ahead of heavy use.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. Our Athens Restoration Co crews extract it, remove what cannot be cleaned and dry the structure to verified readings.
